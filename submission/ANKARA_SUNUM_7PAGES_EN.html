1) Project Summary

Earth Trend Detective is an interactive analysis and visualization platform that answers: What changed, where, by how much, and is the change statistically meaningful? Using NASA open data, it processes Earth-system time series to reveal trends, anomalies, and location comparisons through maps/globes and charts, and converts results into evidence-based presentation/report outputs.

Data source
NASA POWER (1981–present)
Coverage
201-location target (81 provinces of Türkiye + countries/capitals), 36 parameters
Methods
Trend/decade, p-value, 95% CI, Mann–Kendall, anomaly analysis

Problem & Goal

  • Problem: Earth-system data is vast and technical; critical changes (heat, precipitation, radiation, humidity, wind, etc.) are not quickly transformed into comparable, actionable insights.
  • Goal: Show the direction, magnitude, and statistical significance of change for a selected region/period/parameter in a clear way.
  • Audience/Impact: Citizens, local governments, planners; awareness and decision support for agriculture, water, health, and energy.

2) NASA Data Used & Challenge Alignment

  • NASA POWER (Prediction Of Worldwide Energy Resources) API: Daily/parametric time series retrieved and analyzed by location and date range.
  • Example parameter groups (36 parameters): Temperature (T2M, T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN, T2MDEW, T2MWET), precipitation (PRECTOTCORR), humidity (RH2M, QV2M), wind/pressure (WS10M, WS2M, PS), solar/radiation and energy components (ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, CLRSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, ALLSKY_SFC_LW_DWN, etc.), cloud/aerosol-related indicators.
  • Alignment with challenge: Trend detection, location and magnitude, visualization, and statistical significance evidence (p-value, 95% CI, Mann–Kendall).

Additional Data & Resources

  • World Bank Open Data (WDI API): Complementary context indicators (emissions, energy use, renewable share, PM2.5, electricity access, forest area, etc.) processed in `worldbank_indicator_raw`.
  • Tools/Software: Python, Streamlit, Pandas/NumPy/SciPy (statistics), Plotly (visualization), MySQL/PyMySQL (processed data layer), Requests (API access), HTML/CSS report outputs.

3) Solution Architecture & How It Works

Data & Processing

  • NASA POWER API retrieval by location/parameter/date range
  • MySQL raw and processed layers: `locations`, `nasa_power_raw`, `analysis_results`, `worldbank_indicator_raw`
  • Handling of missing/problematic values (e.g., -999) and coverage differences
  • Daily/monthly series and year-month range selection

Analysis & Visualization

  • Trend (per-decade slope), p-value, 95% confidence interval, Mann–Kendall significance test
  • Seasonal and non-seasonal anomaly analysis
  • Country/province averages and period-to-period change comparisons
  • Interactive maps/globes, time series, and comparison charts

Application Flow (Single Main Flow)

  1. Control Panel: Select data source (MySQL/NASA POWER/Simulation), parameter, location, and date range
  2. Map & Overview: Selected point, metrics, and map/globe views
  3. Trends & Statistics: Time series, trend line, anomalies, p-value/95% CI/Mann–Kendall
  4. Comparison & Evidence: Location scans, country/province averages, anomalies, radiation findings
  5. Presentation/Report: Evidence-based output and downloadable/printable report

4) Example Findings & Evidence

  • Trend evidence: For selected location/parameter, per-decade trend, p-value, 95% CI, and Mann–Kendall results are reported together to quantify significance.
  • Spatial comparison: Across the 201-location target, country/province averages and period changes answer “where and by how much”.
  • Anomaly analysis: Seasonal cycles and non-seasonal deviations are separated to highlight unusual periods.
  • Radiation/climate indicators: Changes in solar radiation and related parameters are presented with interpretable benefit-risk context.

Validation, Reliability & Limitations

  • Reliability: Open NASA data, reproducible statistical methods, processed data layer, and stored analysis outputs.
  • Limitations: Satellite/model data may differ from ground observations; missing data/coverage can affect sensitivity; causality must be interpreted carefully.
  • Risk mitigation: Screenshots, screen recordings, and pre-generated HTML/CSV evidence backups prepared for connectivity/API/DB issues.
